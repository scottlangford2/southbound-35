# Southbound 35 — Editorial Policy

*One-page summary of editorial standards and conflict-of-interest rules. Derived from §§6 and 9 of the long-term plan. Shared with collaborators, coauthors, and any party asking the basis on which the blog operates.*

**Maintainer:** W. Scott Langford, Texas State University
**Updated:** June 2026

---

## Quality and sourcing

1. **Every chart cites its source in the chart footer.** Not in surrounding text, not in a parenthetical, not in the replication code. In the chart itself.
2. **Every post that uses non-trivial data points to a public replication package** under `southbound-35/posts/`, committed on the same calendar day as publication.
3. **Illustrative figures are labeled as such** in their source line when the underlying values are approximate or transcribed because no clean machine-readable source is available.
4. **Claims about specific districts, officials, or firms are checked against named primary sources** — TEA, Comptroller, Census, BEA, FDIC, the agency's own adopted budget. Press coverage is a starting point, not a citation.
5. **Errors are corrected in-line with a dated note.** Not silently. The note states what was wrong, when it was changed, and (when relevant) who flagged it.
6. **Posts touching active academic work go through the same coauthor review as the academic work itself.**

## Conflicts of interest

- **Consulting.** No post on a current consulting client's jurisdiction or work product during the engagement, and for six months after. Past engagements are disclosed in any post that discusses the relevant jurisdiction.
- **Research.** The blog does not scoop my own academic papers and is not used to puff them. Academic work appears here only after the underlying paper is in working-paper form or beyond, and a blog post about it is explicit that it is a public-facing summary.
- **Texas State University.** The site footer makes clear that the blog is a personal publication. The university's name and brand are not used as endorsement. Standard academic-freedom protections apply, and so do the standard academic-norm restrictions on partisan activity.

## On naming individuals

The blog will name elected officials, civil servants, and public figures in their official capacities, and will critique **decisions** freely. It will not critique individuals' character or motivations. The rule: **disagree with the decision, not the person who made it.**

## On political affiliation

The blog does not endorse candidates or parties and does not publish commentary that could be mistaken for endorsement. Analytical pieces on local political economy (turnout, ballot structure, MUD board races, school-board races) are in scope. Partisan commentary is not.

## On religion

The religion-lane detours are written from inside a religious worldview and are explicit about this. They do not proselytize and do not assume the reader shares the worldview. A non-religious reader should be able to enjoy the post without feeling preached at.

## Corrections and disputes

If you believe a post contains a factual error, a misattribution, or treats a person or organization unfairly, email <scottlangford@txstate.edu> with the post URL and the specific concern. Substantiated factual corrections are made within 48 hours, in-line, with a dated note.

## On AI assistance

Some posts are drafted with the assistance of large language models (currently Claude, by Anthropic). All factual claims, citations, and analytical conclusions are reviewed and approved by the author before publication. AI assistance does not change the standards above.
