## Why this order

The blog is written from San Marcos. Texas State sits in Hays County. The public-scholarship value of a post is highest where the author can ground-truth the institutional landscape by being physically embedded in it. Each step outward from San Marcos is a step away from that advantage. So the build-out goes:

- **Ring 0 — Hays follow-ons** (the existing series gave the macro picture; there's a lot of unmined ground-level material right here)
- **Ring 1 — directly adjacent**: Comal first (most analogous, biggest story), then Caldwell, Guadalupe, the southern Travis edge, eastern Blanco
- **Ring 2 — beyond adjacent**: Williamson, Bexar Northside, Bastrop / eastern Caldwell

The four Ring-0 posts and the five Ring-1 (Comal) posts below cover roughly the next nine months of weekly cadence, allowing for detour weeks and the pillar-series mechanics posts in between.

# Ring 0 — Hays follow-ons (four posts)

The existing Hays series covered growth, projections, water, schools, and governance at the county level. What it did not do — and what is now the most valuable next move — is go below the county level. Each of these four posts goes one degree deeper into the part of Hays that is closest to the university.

## Post A1 — San Marcos and Texas State: an enrollment-finance feedback loop

The single most distinctive feature of San Marcos's public finance is that the city's largest employer, anchor tenant, and demographic engine is also a state-funded university whose appropriations and tuition revenue are set in Austin, not by the city. The post traces the loop: TXST enrollment growth → student housing demand → property values around the campus → city sales tax (students spending) and hotel/motel tax (parents and event visitors) → city budget capacity → infrastructure investment around the campus → enrollment recruiting environment → next round.

**Data needed:**
- TXST enrollment 1990–present (THECB and university reports)
- San Marcos sales tax monthly remittances (Comptroller)
- HOT (Hotel Occupancy Tax) receipts for San Marcos
- San Marcos adopted budgets, 2010–present
- City of San Marcos certified appraisal roll
- TXST capital expenditure history (the campus master plans)

## Post A2 — Kyle and Buda: the corridor's fastest-growing pair

Kyle and Buda are different stories that get told as one because they sit five miles apart on I-35 and both grew dramatically. The post separates them. Kyle has the older Hispanic-majority center, a larger total population, and a more diversified employment base. Buda is wealthier per capita, denser in retail and warehousing, and tied much more closely to Austin commuting. Their school finance pictures are nearly opposite (Hays CISD splits both); their water-supply postures differ; their bond capacity differs.

**Data needed:**

- Adopted city budgets for both, 2015–present
- LEHD origin-destination commute data for both
- Hays CISD attendance zone maps and capacity numbers

## Post A3 — Three districts, three school-finance worlds

Hays CISD, San Marcos CISD, and Dripping Springs ISD are within fifteen miles of each other and serve fundamentally different communities. Hays CISD is the fast-growing, fast-bonding suburban district. San Marcos CISD is the historically Hispanic-majority, slower-growth, lower-property-wealth district. Dripping Springs ISD is the high-property-wealth, recapture-paying Hill Country district. Showing them side by side is the cleanest way to make the abstract recapture mechanics concrete.

**Data needed:**
- TEA School District Funding reports (all three)
- TEA Recapture annual summaries (Dripping Springs is Chapter 49)
- District-by-district enrollment, racial composition, ED/EL share
- Adopted tax rates by year, all three
- Bond election history

## Post A4 — The western Hays Hill-Country edge

Dripping Springs is the most-discussed jurisdiction in western Hays, but the western edge of the county is its own story. Wimberley, Driftwood, Hays (the town), and the unincorporated wedge bordering Blanco County. Different politics (more Hill-Country libertarian than I-35 suburb), different governance (heavy MUD presence, large unincorporated population), different water supply (Pedernales Electric Cooperative, Hays Trinity Groundwater Conservation District, private wells). The post is a deliberate companion to the Kyle/Buda piece — same county, opposite end, opposite character.

# Ring 1 — Comal County (five-post series)

Comal is the natural Ring-1 first county. It sits directly south of Hays on I-35, shares the Edwards Aquifer system, has a comparable growth trajectory, and is far enough from the Austin anchor to have developed its own employment and tourism base (New Braunfels, Schlitterbahn, Wurstfest, the Comal River). Where Williamson is "another Hays but bigger and tech-heavier," Comal is "another Hays but more constrained by water and more tied to San Antonio."

## Post B1 — The Comal growth story

Same template as the Hays growth post. New Braunfels is the anchor; Bulverde, Garden Ridge, Spring Branch, and the unincorporated edge are the supporting cast. Growth driven by Austin–SA midpoint position, plus a distinctive tourism base.

## Post B2 — Comal projections, with the aquifer caveat

Texas Demographic Center projections, modified by the actual binding constraint that the other corridor counties don't face: Edwards Aquifer Authority pumping permits cap the firm-yield water supply. New Braunfels Utilities has been planning around this for two decades.

## Post B3 — Comal water: the Edwards Aquifer Authority

The single richest material in the Comal series. The EAA permit system (post-1993 Senate Bill 1477), the J17 well as the system's bellwether, the drought-rule trigger schedule, the Habitat Conservation Plan with its endangered-species (Comal Springs salamander) cap, and what all of this means for residential and commercial growth.

## Post B4 — Comal schools and Comal ISD's bond posture

Comal ISD spans almost the entire county (one of the largest by area in Texas). Rapid enrollment growth, a string of large bond passes, distinctive tax-rate management. Note also New Braunfels ISD as the smaller anchor-city district inside Comal ISD's geographic footprint.

## Post B5 — Comal governance

The county's governance topography. Commissioners, New Braunfels (the only city of meaningful size), the heavy MUD presence in Bulverde and on the I-35 corridor, the Edwards Aquifer Authority as a sui generis special district, the GCD. Compare with Hays.
